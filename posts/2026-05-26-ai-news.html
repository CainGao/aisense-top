1. White House Requests $9 Billion for Spy Agency AI Chips

The CIA and NSA lack the computing capacity to run cutting-edge AI models, reports The New York Times. The White House has approved a request for $9 billion to purchase Nvidia Grace Blackwell superchips and build infrastructure for intelligence agencies. Congress still needs to approve the funds, but the request signals how critical AI has become to national security operations.

📰 The New York Times

2. Pope Leo's AI Encyclical Calls for Global Ethical Framework

In his first major encyclical, Pope Leo XIV calls for a comprehensive legal and ethical framework to govern AI development and deployment. The document addresses AI's impact on labor markets, autonomous warfare, and human dignity. TechCrunch notes the encyclical "isn't really about AI" — it's about what it means to be human in a world increasingly shaped by intelligent machines. The Vatican's intervention adds moral weight to an increasingly polarized policy debate.

📰 TechCrunch

Products Launches & Releases

3. ChatGPT for PowerPoint Launches in Beta

OpenAI and Microsoft launched ChatGPT for PowerPoint, a sidebar integration that lets users create and edit presentations using natural language prompts. Users can reference documents, images, and other source material to generate slides. The feature is available now in beta for all ChatGPT plans including Free tier — a aggressive distribution strategy that puts AI directly inside one of the world's most-used productivity tools.

📰 The Verge

4. Google Gemini Omni: The Anything-to-Anything AI Model

Google unveiled Gemini Omni, a new multimodal model capable of transforming any input type into any output type. The Verge's hands-on showed it deepfaking a reporter in front of the Eiffel Tower and sending a kid's stuffed animal rafting in generated video. While impressive, the model raises fresh questions about the line between creative tools and deception — and whether "not quite the singularity" is good enough to matter.

📰 The Verge

Funding Startups & Investment

5. Hark Raises $700M Series A for "Universal" AI Interface

Stealth startup Hark raised a staggering $700 million Series A to build what it calls a "universal AI interface." Details remain scarce, but the sheer size of the round — one of the largest Series A rounds in history — signals that investors believe the next major AI platform will be an abstraction layer that unifies how humans interact with all AI models and tools. The question is whether Hark can deliver on that promise before the incumbents catch up.
