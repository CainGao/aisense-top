1. Uber President Says AI Spending Is Getting "Harder to Justify"

Uber's president raised eyebrows by publicly questioning whether massive AI investments are generating sufficient returns. Speaking at a tech conference, he noted that while AI capabilities are impressive, the gap between what companies spend on AI infrastructure and what they earn back is widening. The comments echo growing skepticism from enterprise leaders about the ROI of generative AI — and suggest the current spending boom may face a reckoning.

2. Erin Brockovich Maps Every AI Data Center in America

Environmental activist Erin Brockovich launched an interactive map logging every known AI data center project across the United States, along with community complaints about energy use, water consumption, and noise. The map reveals patterns of rapid expansion into residential areas and growing local opposition. As AI infrastructure booms, the environmental and social costs are becoming impossible to ignore — and Brockovich's map puts them all in one place.

3. Google's AI Overviews Can't Tell if 2027 Is Next Year

Google's AI Overviews feature is occasionally telling users that 2027 is two years away when they ask "is it 2027 next year?" The error stems from the AI citing old social media posts and Reddit threads that were themselves making fun of wrong answers. Google's AI search continues to struggle with basic factual queries even as the company expands its deployment. Meanwhile, searching the word "disregard" still breaks the feature entirely.

4. Nobody Wants to Say Why They Only Listen to Suno AI Music

Suno's AI music generator is producing tracks that users prefer over human-made music — and nobody wants to talk about why. The Verge reports a growing community of people who have abandoned streaming services entirely, listening exclusively to AI-generated songs tailored to their preferences. The trend raises uncomfortable questions about the future of music as an art form, and whether personalized AI slop is the final form of audio entertainment.

5. India's Gig Economy Is Training the World's Robots

Startup Human Archive is tapping into India's massive services economy to collect physical-world training data for AI and robotics companies. The company partners with gig workers across India to gather real-world interaction data — from picking up objects to navigating spaces — that's essential for training embodied AI systems. The model raises questions about labor practices in the AI supply chain, but demonstrates how the global AI industry increasingly depends on low-cost human labor in developing nations.
